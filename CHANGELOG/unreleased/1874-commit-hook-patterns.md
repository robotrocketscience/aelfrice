### Fixed

- **The commit hook reads only a lowercase relation on one line ([#1874](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/1874)).** The hook ignored case in the verb, so an uppercase edge-type name used as a noun, such as "the SUPERSEDES writer", wrote a false edge. Its matches also crossed line breaks, so a commit's subject and body could join into one belief. The verb now has to match in lowercase, so "SUPERSEDES" and "Supersedes" no longer count. Each match stays on one line of the message. A phrase that wraps to the next line is cut at the line break, and a relation is lost when the break touches the verb. A match with a side made only of function words now stores nothing, so "This supersedes the old parser", "the new parser supersedes that", and "and cites the memo" write no belief or edge. Function words are determiners, pronouns, relative words, quantifiers, conjunctions, prepositions, auxiliaries and modals, negators, and adverbs such as "also" and "just". Those phrases had one belief id across all commits, so they collected edges from unrelated commits. A word of two or more letters in all capitals, such as "IT" or "WHO", counts as a name and stores. A capitalized word that matches the list, such as the code names "None" and "Any" or the name "Will", doesn't store. A word with a hyphen or underscore inside it, such as "one-shot" or "all_of", counts as one word and stores. A side can't start after an apostrophe, so "it's" no longer stores "s". The words stored from a match can still fail to form a complete phrase, such as "the old". Beliefs and edges already in your store don't change: their log rows hold the stored phrases, so they replay as they were written, and `aelf doctor` doesn't report them as out of date. The comment in the `post-commit` block now says the hook stores the words on each side of a phrase, not the commit's message, and `aelf setup` updates it the next time it runs in a repository. Retrieval's query building and the entity index don't change.
