### Documentation

- **`benchmarks/retrieval_metrics.py` no longer claims its metrics are monotone in the token budget ([#1574](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/1574)).** The module reasoned that "retrieval fills the budget in rank order, so cutting the budget truncates the tail", and concluded that every metric is monotone non-decreasing in the budget. `clustering.pack_with_clusters` does not truncate: stage 1 abandons on the first representative it cannot afford, and stage 2 skips an over-budget belief and keeps filling. The budget therefore **selects** rather than truncating, and raising it by one token can evict a belief the lower budget admitted. With the gold-bearing belief cheap and ranked second, that takes `recall_at_k` and `reciprocal_rank` from 1.0 to 0.0. The skip-and-continue fill is deliberate — it maximises how many beliefs a fixed budget delivers — so the docstring now records the non-monotonicity as an accepted property rather than a defect, and names the tests that pin each half. The guard that appeared to cover this was `test_shrinking_the_budget_never_raises_a_metric`, which truncates a list and never sets a budget, prices a belief, or calls the packer; it is renamed to `test_keeping_fewer_items_never_raises_a_metric`, and the budget claim now has a test of its own asserting the opposite.
