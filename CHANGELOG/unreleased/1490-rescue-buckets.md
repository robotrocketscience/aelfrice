### Changed

- **The transcript-admission harness reports the rescued set split by kind ([#1490](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/1490)).** `rescued_distinct` said how many sentences the relaxed filter admits and nothing about what kind of thing they were, and the kinds behave very differently once stored: a durable policy statement earns its slot, while an ephemeral session-status line ("No failures yet.") is true of one run and of nothing afterwards — yet both enter through `derive()`'s `role=user` transcript path at the undeflated `USER_SOURCE` prior ([#888](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/888)), storing as `factual`/`user_transcript` at `alpha=3.0, beta=1.0` (posterior mean 0.75, origin rank 3), so they compete with durable policy for the same injection slots. `benchmarks/transcript_noise_admission.py` now buckets into `ephemeral_status`, `operator_directive`, `durable_policy` and `unclassified`. Nothing reaches the ingest write path. A shape detector *would* have reached it, and that option was rejected twice — on 2026-08-11 and again on 2026-08-12 — as "the same widening #1371 §1 just narrowed". A wrong bucket therefore produces a wrong number in a manually-run report, and never a dropped belief. `unclassified` is the deliverable rather than a leftover — CI can never watch these counts, because the corpus is an untracked local archive, so a rise there is the only available signal that the closed set of three has stopped describing what the filter admits. `tests/test_transcript_noise_buckets_1490.py` pins that the split is total (a sentence matching no rule is counted, not dropped), that every declared bucket is reachable (a dead bucket is a permanent zero column that reads as a measurement), and that an unseen shape reaches `unclassified` instead of being absorbed. The disclosure comment in `src/aelfrice/noise_filter.py` is stamped with **both** figures and is explicit that the finding is no longer reproducible: the measurement of record is 6 distinct / 9 rows over 17,628 sentences at `corpus_sha256 3ad68670…` (tree `c6f0c0c3`, 2026-08-12), and a re-run on 2026-08-19 yields 0 / 0 over 18 sentences at `corpus_sha256 cffcf557…` because the transcript archive was removed from disk around 2026-08-13 — not by rotation, since `transcript_logger` contains no pruning code. Quoting only the fresh run would report a degenerate corpus as the finding; quoting only the old one would imply it is still checkable.
