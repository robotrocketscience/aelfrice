### Documentation

- **The UserPromptSubmit payload is bounded per block, and that is now written where the bound is ([#1560](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/1560)).** [#1551](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/1551) bounded the `<aelfrice-memory>` envelope and [#1552](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/pull/1552) scoped the claim to it; what was left open was whether one bound governs the whole payload. It does not. `HOOK_BLOCK_TOKEN_CEILING`'s docstring now states the contract positively: every block a fire writes names its own bound, no bound spans them, and there is deliberately no payload ceiling. The two bounded blocks and the two mechanisms that enforce them are named — the `<aelfrice-memory>` envelope by this constant through `enforce_block_ceiling`, hard with the #379 lock exemption, since that function never drops a `lock="user"` element and a block whose locks alone are oversized is emitted over the ceiling with a note on stderr; the `<cadence-checkpoint>` block by `DEFAULT_REBUILDER_TOKEN_BUDGET` through the rebuilder's pack loop, **soft**. Calling the two equivalent would be false: [#1546](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/1546) already records that `<retrieved-beliefs budget_used="N/M">` can report `N > M` on the rebuild lane, and the measured fire below is an instance of it rather than a citation of it: that block's own `budget_used` attribute reports 22106 characters packed against a budget of 16000, and the block comes to 5813 estimated tokens against a budget of 4000.

  **Why per block, and not one ceiling.** The shed order removes prompt-independent lanes before the prompt's own retrieval hits, so extending it across the payload would make the hook delete retrieved beliefs to make room for a rebuild recap. That cross-block trade is unmeasured and no cross-block shed is added here; the rebuilder's own budget stays with #1546.

  **What the rationale must not say, because it is false: that the trade has never happened.** An earlier revision of this entry and of the docstring said "nobody has measured whether that is the right trade" without qualifying it, and inside the `<aelfrice-memory>` envelope the trade already runs — for the `<cadence-resume>` recap (#871), not for the cadence checkpoint. The recap is prepended to the session-start sub-block and emitted inside the envelope, so it is charged to this ceiling, **and the dropper sheds it**: `_maybe_read_cadence_resume` wraps a body the context rebuilder rendered, whose `<belief>` elements are ordinary droppable elements, and only the `<cadence-resume>` wrapper is exempt — by not being a `<belief>` element at all. A lock inside the recap is not exempt either, because the rebuilder writes `locked="true"` where the dropper reads `lock="user"`. And because the recap sits outside `<core>` and `<recent-work>`, `_ceiling_drop_order` buckets its elements with the per-turn hits, at the *head* of a bucket that is shed tail-first — so the prompt's own hits go before the recap is touched. `scripts/measure_block_ceiling.py --resume-drop` measures it on a real P1 resume cache against a 40-lock / 20-core / 20-hit store: the recap keeps 65 `<belief>` elements untrimmed and 34 after the ceiling, while prompt-matched beliefs reaching the model fall from 6 without a recap to 0 with one, counting an element and a `seen` pointer alike so #1547's dedupe is not miscounted as a loss. The per-block ruling is unchanged; what changed is that its stated reason no longer rests on a false claim, and the code's actual position is named — the within-envelope trade happens, the cross-block one does not.

  **`_ceiling_drop_order` claimed the opposite, and this branch is what disproves it.** Its docstring said "elements outside both named sections are the per-turn hits by construction" — a #1552 claim, true of `<locked>` and false of the recap. It now says so, and says that the consequence is an ordering the function's own first paragraph argues against: the prompt-independent recap outranks the prompt's own hits. Re-bucketing it would change what the hook injects, and #1560 is a documentation ruling, so the behaviour is recorded rather than altered.

  **The sum is a measured figure, from a committed producer.** `scripts/measure_block_ceiling.py --cadence` fires one store of user locks, `<core>` beliefs and prompt-matching hits — sized by the `CADENCE_LOCKS` / `CADENCE_CORE` / `CADENCE_HITS` constants it seeds from — with every writer live, and reports each writer's share. The writers are enumerated from `user_prompt_submit` itself rather than from the two that carry a budget: the cadence write; the memory envelope through `_write_memory_block`, whose two call sites are the mutually exclusive retrieval and `elif gate_skip:` branches, so at most one of them fires; and the two phantom notes, `<aelfrice-phantom-opportunity>` (#980) and `<aelfrice-phantom-promotion-opportunity>` (#1132), which carry no token budget of any kind — each is bounded only by a per-session fire budget and a per-entry topic length. That fire emits 11926 estimated tokens on stdout: 5813 of cadence checkpoint, 5898 of memory envelope inside its ceiling of 6000, 99 and 116 of the two notes. The payload is roughly twice the ceiling while the block the ceiling governs is inside it, which is the arithmetic the contract makes explicit. `--emit-figures` publishes each of those sizes, and the two bounds they are read against, so CI re-derives them, and the arm refuses to publish a total from a fire that was missing a writer, whose memory envelope overran its own ceiling, or whose payload did not exceed it. **Those figures exclude the `<cadence-resume>` recap**, and the arm now refuses to print them if that stops being true: the recap is read from a cache a prior session's Stop-side cadence fire wrote, and this arm's work directory is fresh, so nothing is prepended. The recap's own charge is `--resume-drop`'s subject, not this sum's.

  **The contract is tested at the emit boundary, on the payload the two candidate contracts disagree about.** `tests/test_hook_payload_per_block_bound_1560.py` drives the real hook entrypoint and asserts on captured stdout, not on either block in isolation: a payload whose blocks are each inside their own bound and whose sum is outside `HOOK_BLOCK_TOKEN_CEILING` is emitted whole. A test that checked each block separately would pass under a single payload ceiling too and would pin nothing. A second test fires the same store with cadence on and off, on a fixture whose memory block is over its ceiling so the trim is live, and requires the emitted envelope to be byte-identical across the pair — which beliefs the ceiling deletes cannot depend on whether a sibling block shares the payload. `tests/test_hook_ceiling_cadence_resume_1560.py` pins the other half: that the dropper sheds a recap's `<belief>` elements, spares only its wrapper, buckets them behind the prompt's own hits, and that a recap costs the envelope prompt-matched beliefs the same fire reaches without one. Those four claims were wrong in prose for exactly as long as nothing asserted them.

  **The exposure is cadence-enabled-only.** `[cadence] enabled` is unset by default, so `_maybe_run_ups_cadence_checkpoint` returns `None`, no Stop-side fire writes a resume cache, and a stock install gets neither the second block nor a recap; the measured fire enables that lane and both phantom lanes explicitly, and a test records the default-off absence — by *omitting* the `[cadence]` section rather than writing `enabled = false`, since an explicit off pins a setting instead of the shipped default. This is not something every user is exposed to. It is, however, the configuration behind the largest envelope rows [#1547](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/1547) measured in the field, which is why the sum is worth having as a figure.

  **AC2 is `NOT_APPLICABLE`, recorded rather than left unchecked.** The issue's second criterion — "if one bound: the shed order is stated and tested across blocks" — is conditional on the option that was not chosen. Under the per-block ruling there is no cross-block shed order to state or test, so it is marked not applicable, on the #1160 convention that an n/a criterion says so rather than going quietly unmet. No payload-tokens field was added to the hook audit record and no stderr threshold note was added; both were considered and not selected.
