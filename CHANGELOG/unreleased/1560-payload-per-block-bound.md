### Documentation

- **The UserPromptSubmit payload is bounded per block, and that is now written where the bound is ([#1560](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/1560)).** [#1551](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/1551) bounded the `<aelfrice-memory>` envelope and [#1552](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/pull/1552) scoped the claim to it; what was left open was whether one bound governs the whole payload. It does not. `HOOK_BLOCK_TOKEN_CEILING`'s docstring now states the contract positively: every block a fire writes names its own bound, no bound spans them, and there is deliberately no payload ceiling. The two bounded blocks and the two mechanisms that enforce them are named — the `<aelfrice-memory>` envelope by this constant through `enforce_block_ceiling`, hard; the `<cadence-checkpoint>` block by `DEFAULT_REBUILDER_TOKEN_BUDGET` through the rebuilder's pack loop, **soft**. Calling the two equivalent would be false: [#1546](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/1546) already records that `<retrieved-beliefs budget_used="N/M">` can report `N > M` on the rebuild lane, and the measured fire below shows a checkpoint block of 5813 estimated tokens against that budget of 4000.

  **Why per block, and not one ceiling.** The shed order removes prompt-independent lanes before the prompt's own retrieval hits, so extending it across the payload would make the hook delete retrieved beliefs to make room for a rebuild recap. Nobody has measured whether that is the right trade, so no cross-block shed is added here; the rebuilder's own budget stays with #1546.

  **The sum is a measured figure, from a committed producer.** `scripts/measure_block_ceiling.py --cadence` fires one store of user locks, `<core>` beliefs and prompt-matching hits — sized by the `CADENCE_LOCKS` / `CADENCE_CORE` / `CADENCE_HITS` constants it seeds from — with every writer live, and reports each writer's share. The writers are enumerated from `user_prompt_submit` itself rather than from the two that carry a budget: the cadence write; the memory envelope through `_write_memory_block`, whose two call sites are the mutually exclusive retrieval and `elif gate_skip:` branches, so at most one of them fires; and the two phantom notes, `<aelfrice-phantom-opportunity>` (#980) and `<aelfrice-phantom-promotion-opportunity>` (#1132), which carry no token budget of any kind — each is bounded only by a per-session fire budget and a per-entry topic length. That fire emits 11926 estimated tokens on stdout: 5813 of cadence checkpoint, 5898 of memory envelope inside its ceiling of 6000, 99 and 116 of the two notes. The payload is roughly twice the ceiling while the block the ceiling governs is inside it, which is the arithmetic the contract makes explicit. `--emit-figures` publishes each of those sizes, and the two bounds they are read against, so CI re-derives them, and the arm refuses to publish a total from a fire that was missing a writer, whose memory envelope overran its own ceiling, or whose payload did not exceed it.

  **The contract is tested at the emit boundary, on the payload the two candidate contracts disagree about.** `tests/test_hook_payload_per_block_bound_1560.py` drives the real hook entrypoint and asserts on captured stdout, not on either block in isolation: a payload whose blocks are each inside their own bound and whose sum is outside `HOOK_BLOCK_TOKEN_CEILING` is emitted whole. A test that checked each block separately would pass under a single payload ceiling too and would pin nothing. A second test fires the same store with cadence on and off, on a fixture whose memory block is over its ceiling so the trim is live, and requires the emitted envelope to be byte-identical across the pair — which beliefs the ceiling deletes cannot depend on whether a sibling block shares the payload.

  **The exposure is cadence-enabled-only.** `[cadence] enabled` is unset by default, so `_maybe_run_ups_cadence_checkpoint` returns `None` and a stock install never writes the second block; the measured fire enables that lane and both phantom lanes explicitly, and a test records the default-off absence. This is not something every user is exposed to. It is, however, the configuration behind the largest envelope rows [#1547](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/1547) measured in the field, which is why the sum is worth having as a figure.

  **AC2 is `NOT_APPLICABLE`, recorded rather than left unchecked.** The issue's second criterion — "if one bound: the shed order is stated and tested across blocks" — is conditional on the option that was not chosen. Under the per-block ruling there is no cross-block shed order to state or test, so it is marked not applicable, on the #1160 convention that an n/a criterion says so rather than going quietly unmet. No payload-tokens field was added to the hook audit record and no stderr threshold note was added; both were considered and not selected.
