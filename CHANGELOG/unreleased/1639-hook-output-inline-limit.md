### Fixed

- **A hook's whole output now fits the host's inline limit, so the model reads all of it and every user lock is either shown or named ([#1639](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/1639)).** The host inlines at most 10,000 characters of a hook's output. Past that it saves the output to a file and puts only a 2,000-character preview into the model's context ([hooks reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks.md)). aelfrice's block ceiling, 6,000 estimated tokens (about 24,000 characters) with user locks exempt, sat above that limit. `scripts/hook_output_census.py` counted 189 of 456 aelfrice hook outputs in local transcripts as saved rather than inlined. Of the 79 saved outputs whose full text carried user locks, 36 lost at least one lock from the preview the model saw. Counting only outputs written since 2026-09-24, 17 of 70 were still saved, with a median of 13.7 KB. A new bound, `HOOK_PAYLOAD_CHAR_LIMIT` (9,500 characters), now covers everything one `UserPromptSubmit` or `SessionStart` fire writes to stdout, and the `additionalContext` the `PreToolUse` search hook writes. The command-result note, the cadence checkpoint, and both phantom notes are charged first, and the memory envelope gets the room they leave. The two phantom notes share what is left after a 4,000-character reserve for the envelope, so they cannot take it. An opportunity that does not fit is not recorded as shown: a promotion, or a new contradiction, surfaces on a later turn, and a gap or new-entity note, which belongs to its prompt, can fire again when a prompt raises it. The notes record what they showed only after they are written. The envelope trims to that room in the shed order it already used: a `<cadence-resume>` recap, then `<core>`, then `<recent-work>`, then the per-turn hits. Before any of those, an envelope over its room drops `seen` pointers to beliefs an earlier turn showed, whose text is already in the context window, and then the `<recent-work>` lane. User locks still come first and are still never trimmed mid-content. When the locks alone do not fit, the block keeps the locks that fit in list order and cuts the rest whole. A cut lock takes every render with it: its element or the one-line `ref` entry of a reference-tier lock, its `seen` pointer, and its element inside a `<cadence-resume>` recap. The envelope decides which locks the room holds before it sheds anything, so the per-turn hits never make room for a lock that is then cut. The block then ends with one line that names the cut locks (up to 20 ids, then a count) and points to `aelf locked`. stderr also says how many were cut, so no lock disappears silently. A cut lock takes no exposure or audit record, because the model never saw it, and the search hook's telemetry counts only the lines it showed. A cadence checkpoint packs to the room left after a 4,000-character reserve for the envelope. The post-compaction rebuild block gets the room the baseline leaves. When their pack loop overruns, the checkpoint and the rebuild block are cut at element boundaries first, then at a line boundary with a marker that says the block was cut. The SessionStart recap line is charged before either block, and the search hook drops its L1 lines before any lock. Each field of the `<recent-work>` lane (the branch, the upstream, and each commit subject) is capped at 200 characters as rendered, and a `#N` reference longer than 7 digits isn't listed: that lane is repository text the trim can't shed. This reverses the #1560 ruling that no bound would span more than one block, on evidence that ruling did not have: the host's limit applies to the fire's whole output, and a sum of per-block bounds cannot guarantee it. `AELFRICE_HOOK_BLOCK_CEILING=0` still lifts the envelope's ceiling and its share of the bound; the cadence checkpoint and the rebuild block stay packed to the room.
