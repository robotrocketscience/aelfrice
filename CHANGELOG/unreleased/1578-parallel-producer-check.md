### Performance

- **The published-figure gate re-runs its producers concurrently, so its cost is the slowest one rather than the sum ([#1578](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/1578)).** `check_producers` spawns one child per store-free producer ([#1307](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/1307)) and ran them one after another. On an M-series laptop with an SSD, the five producers on this tree cost 15.0s (`benchmarks/injection_budget_bytes.py`), 10.9s (`benchmarks/store_open_cost.py`), 10.6s (`scripts/measure_block_ceiling.py`), 2.4s (`scripts/budget_discriminability_census.py`) and 0.4s (`benchmarks/published_constants.py`) — 39.2s serial against the 120s `pytest-timeout` marker on `test_the_repo_passes_the_producer_checks`, which duly timed out on the v5.0.0 release pull request ([#1576](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/1576)) and passed on a re-run. Two of those producers were new in v5.0.0, so the check got about 40% more expensive in one release with nothing warning about it. The children are independent by construction — one process each, each emitting its own figures on its own stdout, each building scratch under its own `mkdtemp` — so they now run on a `ThreadPoolExecutor` over `subprocess.run`, one worker per producer up to `PRODUCER_MAX_WORKERS`. The same five now take 18.1s. Raising the marker instead was rejected: it works today and has to be raised again on the next producer, and nothing makes that visible until a release cut.

  **Completion order does not reach the output.** A gate whose failure list is ordered by whichever child finished first prints a different diff on every run, and a reviewer cannot tell a new failure from a reshuffled one — worse than the timeout it replaces. So the pool is submitted in producer order, `map` returns in submission order, and every `Report.fail` is made afterwards on one thread walking that same order: `run_producer` runs off-thread and decides nothing, `report_producer` decides everything and runs on the main thread. Checked against the real tree with one figure broken per producer: two runs over the same tree emit byte-identical stdout and stderr, with the five producer failures in sorted producer order. `tests/test_derived_figures_1469.py` proves both halves on fake producers — one test runs four 0.75s sleepers and asserts the concurrent wall is under half the serial one, and one gives the fakes descending sleeps so they finish in the exact reverse of producer order, asserts they really did, and then asserts two runs print the same list in producer order. Forcing `max_workers = 1` in `run_producers` reds both.

  The 120s marker is **kept**, and kept at 120s, as a hang detector rather than a budget: the same five producers cost 39.3s through the gate serially against 18.3s concurrently, and the test as a whole — text scan included — now takes 20.5s, about 5.8x under the cap where it was about 2.9x. Lowering it to track the new wall clock would hand back the margin this change bought — a hosted runner is furthest from a laptop on the disk-bound axis `store_open_cost.py` leans on, since it walks a three-size grid up to a 20,000-belief store. To price a producer before adding it rather than at a release cut, `scripts/check_derived_figures.py --time-producers` prints each producer's own wall time, the serial sum and the concurrent wall; the table above is its output and is recorded in the `check_producers` docstring. The number to watch is the new producer's own time, because the wall clock is now the maximum: anything slower than the current 15.0s worst case moves it.
