### Documentation

- **`docs/user/CONFIG.md` no longer presents `posterior_weight` as a confidence signal ([#1592](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/1592)).** The key blends `log(posterior_mean)` into the L1 BM25 score, and a reader would reasonably expect that posterior to reflect how a belief has performed. On a real store it does not: `(α, β)` is set at ingest from the belief's type and source, and afterwards moves only when you act on the belief — `aelf feedback`, `aelf confirm`, the propagation of one of those, a manual `aelf clamp-ghosts` sweep, or the sentiment lane that ships off. Retrieval exposure used to add `+0.1` per surfacing until [#1086](https://github.com/robotrocketscience/aelfrice/issues/1086) made it audit-only in July 2026, having measured that it promoted junk above real knowledge, so a constantly retrieved belief now gains nothing by design. Measured across 26 stores and 172,089 beliefs with `benchmarks/posterior_prior_grid_sweep.py`, 4.08% carry a posterior that has moved from its insertion value and 96.3% of those moved before that change — fewer than 300 beliefs on the whole host were moved by the feedback path as it currently ships. The docs now say what follows for tuning: raising the weight makes retrieval more **type-aware**, promoting whatever the classifier typed as a requirement or correction and whatever came from a user source, rather than more confidence-aware; and the clustering onto a handful of posterior values that sets the freeze point low is this effect, not a quirk of a particular corpus. The section names the commands that do move a posterior, and records that the lock floor refuses passive feedback on a locked belief so correcting one takes `aelf unlock` first.
